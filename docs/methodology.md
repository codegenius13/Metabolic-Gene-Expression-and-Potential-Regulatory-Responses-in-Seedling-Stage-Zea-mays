Downloaded the B73 leaf RNA‑seq FASTQ files from NCBI (SRA accession SRP080208) using SRA Toolkit for control and four stress conditions (salinity, drought, heat, cold; two replicates each).
Ran FastQC and MultiQC on raw reads to inspect quality, adapter content, GC distribution. Trimmed adapters and low-quality bases using fastp. Ensured that post-QC metrics are acceptable, similar to Li et al. (who reported very high‐quality clean data).
Obtained the maize B73 reference genome (AGPv4) and GTF annotation. Aligned cleaned reads with a splice-aware aligner using HISAT2 and then counted reads per gene with featureCounts. Inspected mapping rates and sample correlations. Performed a PCA or heatmap on samples to confirm replicates cluster and stresses separately.
Used DESeq2 in R to compare each stress vs. control. Applied the same cutoffs as Li et al. for consistency (adjusted p ≤0.05, |log₂FC|≥1). This yielded four DEG sets (Salinity, Cold, Drought, Heat). Confirmed DE counts are of similar magnitude (thousands of DEGs per stress).
Used maize B73 RefGen4 gene annotations and GO molecular-function to define DE metabolic enzymes genes. This approach was used for our practice ideas but Li et al. used direct KEGG pathway mapping
For each enriched GO biological process, gene-level log₂ fold changes from DESeq2 were used to calculate the numbers and proportions of upregulated and downregulated genes, together with the median log₂ fold change. Using a 70% directional threshold to define “coordinated” responses (≥70% of DEGs upregulated or downregulated were coordinated, responses were classified as mixed when neither direction reached the 70% threshold).
The directional summaries were integrated into a process-by-stress matrix to compare shared, stress-specific, and opposing transcriptional patterns across drought, salinity, heat, and cold. Heatmaps and summary plots were generated using R and Python, while process-overlap tables were used to identify GO terms enriched across multiple stresses or restricted to individual stress conditions. GO enrichment statistics supported the interpretation of biological-process overrepresentation, while cross-stress direction summaries provided complementary evidence about expression patterns.
Examined the DESeq2 count object using library-size summaries, PCA, Pearson sample-correlation analysis, and Euclidean sample-distance analysis. Performed PCA using the 500 most variable genes to visualize sample-level expression variation. Used correlation and distance heatmaps to assess sample similarity and replicate consistency.
Differential expression results generated using DESeq2 were integrated with the RefGen4 metabolic enzyme gene annotations and the GO biological-process enrichment results. Using R and Python filtered genes based on the established significance criteria (adjusted p ≤ 0.05 and |log₂ fold change| ≥ 1), then ranked using statistical significance, magnitude and direction of expression change, and their membership in enriched metabolic processes. Cross-stress comparison and pathway-level direction summaries were used to identify genes associated with shared, stress-specific, predominantly upregulated, or predominantly downregulated.
Transcription-factor genes were also identified using PlantTFDB annotations and integrated with the DESeq2 results to determine TF genes showing significant expression changes under each stress condition.
